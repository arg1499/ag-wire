The Rangers' season just left on a stretcher

Igor Shesterkin, helped off the ice. The Vezina winner is the franchise. There is no Plan B.

October 10, 2026 · 9:15 AM ET

Igor Shesterkin, helped off the ice in the second period. The 2022 Vezina winner, 30 years old, the entire foundation of the franchise — down in a loss, and the building went silent in the way buildings do when everyone does the same math at once.

Here's the math: the Rangers are a goalie-dependent team in a league that punishes goalie-dependent teams. Shesterkin doesn't just stop pucks; he erases structural problems — the defensive lapses, the inconsistent five-on-five play, all of it gets laundered through a .930 save percentage. Take him out and you're not looking at a worse version of the Rangers. You're looking at a different, much more ordinary team.

Meanwhile, in Colorado, Cale Makar signed for $163.2 million — the first $20 million player in NHL history. That's the cap era's new math: your best player gets paid like a small country, and you build around the number. The Rangers' math is simpler and scarier: no Shesterkin, no season. Everything else is commentary.

Why it matters: Cup windows are goalie windows. If this is weeks, it's a slump. If it's months, it's a lost year — and a front office that has to answer why a contender had no credible insurance behind the most important player on the roster.
