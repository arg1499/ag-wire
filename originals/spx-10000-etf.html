Wall Street's newest product is a coin flip

A new ETF offers an all-or-nothing bet on the S&P 500 hitting 10,000. The line between investing and gambling keeps moving.

October 10, 2026 · 9:10 AM ET

There's a new ETF that offers investors an all-or-nothing bet on the S&P 500 reaching 10,000. Not a tilt. Not leveraged exposure. All or nothing — the index gets there, or the money's gone.

MarketWatch's verdict: Wall Street keeps blurring the line between investing and gambling. That's polite. What's actually happening is the ETF industry has discovered that the most lucrative product isn't diversification — it's dopamine. The 0DTE options boom proved retail will pay up for lottery tickets dressed as investments. Now the wrapper is an ETF, the most respectable clothing finance owns.

Don't misunderstand: there's nothing wrong with a consenting adult making a binary bet. The problem is what it signals. When the hottest product launches are structured like casino wagers, it tells you where the marginal dollar is going — not toward patient capital formation, but toward the financial equivalent of a scratch ticket. Every cycle's top is marked by products like this. They're never the cause. They're the tell.

Why it matters: Watch product launches, not just prices. The S&P at 10,000 isn't analysis — it's a round number engineered to trigger FOMO. When Wall Street's innovation budget goes to building better slot machines instead of better portfolios, believe what it's telling you about the clientele.
