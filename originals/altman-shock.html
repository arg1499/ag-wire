Oregon got blindsided too

Dana Altman retires, and his own players call it 'a shock.' A season now starts with a eulogy.

October 10, 2026 · 9:15 AM ET

Dana Altman retires, and his own players call it 'a shock.' A season now starts with a eulogy.

Dana Altman is retiring as the winningest coach in Oregon basketball history, and his players found out the way everyone does now — blindsided. "A shock," in their words. A program days into preseason prep just lost its head coach, its system, and its entire recruiting pitch in a single announcement, less than a month before the season.

This is the part nobody talks about with sudden retirements: the players didn't choose this. They committed to a coach, a scheme, a developmental plan. They'll now play for someone else's vision, or scatter into the portal, or both. Altman's timing — strange on its face — is devastating in its specifics. October is when rotations get set and freshmen learn where to be. Instead, Eugene is holding auditions.

The tributes will be warm and deserved. Altman built Oregon into a national program. But legacies don't run practices, and the Ducks' season now begins with the weirdest possible energy: mourning a departure nobody saw coming while trying to win games next month.

Why it matters: Watch the portal. A coaching shock this close to tip-off doesn't just cost a season — it costs a roster. Whoever takes over inherits players who didn't sign up for them, in a sport where that sentence now ends with transfer paperwork.
