The Finals nobody drew up

A second-year expansion team just swept the champs. The WNBA's superteam era is over.

October 10, 2026 · 9:15 AM ET

A second-year expansion team just swept the champs. The WNBA's superteam era is over.

The Valkyries are going to the WNBA Finals. In year two. As an expansion team. They swept the defending champion Aces — closed it out 87-77 in overtime — and became the quickest expansion franchise in league history to reach the Finals.

On the other side, Angel Reese dropped 22 as the Dream swept the Liberty 85-83 in Game 3 to punch their own ticket. "Job is not done," Reese said. Correct. But also: look at what's done. The Finals are a two-year-old franchise versus a team that finally broke through. No superteam. No dynasty. No inevitability.

This is the thing about expansion done right: the Valkyries didn't sneak in. They swept the champions. That's not parity arriving gradually — that's parity kicking the door in. The WNBA spent years as a league where you could pencil in the finalists before the season. Pencil's broken now.

Why it matters: The league's competitive balance is its best product argument. Casual fans don't tune in for coronations; they tune in for chaos. This Finals is chaos, and it's the most interesting thing the WNBA has staged in years.
