The Yankees' winter of reckoning

Swept by the Rays. Judge watching from the dugout. The hot-seat debate is asking the wrong question.

October 10, 2026 · 9:15 AM ET

Swept by the Rays. Judge watching from the dugout. The hot-seat debate is asking the wrong question.

Swept. By the Rays. In the Bronx, with the familiar October scene: someone else celebrating on their field. Aaron Judge sat in the home dugout afterward, stuck around to watch it, "just hating every single moment of it."

Now comes the hot-seat industrial complex. Boone. Cashman. The whole apparatus. And here's the uncomfortable truth: this isn't a managerial problem anymore. When the same movie plays three Octobers running — the bats going quiet, the pitching thinning out, the other dugout dogpiling — the common denominator isn't the guy filling out the lineup card. It's the roster.

Firing the manager is the easy move. It's also the wrong diagnosis. The Yankees' October failures are structural: a lineup built for the regular season's long math that keeps failing the short series' cruel variance, a rotation that never has quite enough behind the ace, a farm system that produces trade chips instead of contributors.

Why it matters: The question this winter isn't whether changes come — they will. It's whether the Yankees finally admit the construction is the problem, or fire another manager and run it back with a new face on the same flawed blueprint. Judge is 34. The window isn't closed. But it's not propped open, either.
